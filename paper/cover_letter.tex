Dear Editors,

Please consider the Research Article ``Geometric Decomposition of Chronic
Stroke Registration Asymmetry: Numerical Stability, Registration-Pipeline Sensitivity, and Aphasia
Associations in the Aphasia Recovery Cohort'' for publication in
\emph{Imaging Neuroscience}.

This study addresses a recurring interpretive problem in lesion neuroimaging:
registration fields near chronic lesions can be scientifically useful without
being physical measurements of tissue displacement or pressure. We introduce a
contralateral-normalized, lesional-only deformation proxy, construct a
positive-Jacobian stationary log-velocity embedding, and apply an explicitly
bounded Helmholtz--Hodge decomposition. Every log-domain case is audited by
exponentiating the estimated velocity and comparing the reconstruction with its
input deformation.

We additionally repeat the complete analysis using ANTs/SyN forward and inverse
mappings with the symmetric MNI152 template. This pipeline-sensitivity
analysis includes predefined overlap, Jacobian, and inverse-consistency checks,
cross-algorithm descriptor agreement, and identical-split predictive
comparisons. Participant-level transforms remain outside the public repository;
all aggregate results and a second standalone supplement are regenerated from
versioned code.

The study's central result is a bounded methodological advance rather than an
overstated biomarker claim. The log-domain descriptors were numerically valid
for every primary case and highly rank-stable across eight perturbations of
smoothing, taper, padding, and grid resolution. In 210 participants from the
openly available Aphasia Recovery Cohort, deformation and component balance
tracked aphasia severity descriptively, but no association survived adjustment
for conventional clinical and lesion variables and multiplicity. A small
deformation-feature advantage met the within-pipeline prediction criterion in
the ANTs/MNI152 analysis but not in SVReg; descriptor agreement and direct
same-split comparisons bound this as pipeline-sensitive evidence. Hodge
features added no predictive benefit in either pipeline. The complete
nonsignificant and sensitivity results are reported in the Supplementary
Material. This pattern defines both the reproducible information in the
registration geometry and its current limit as an independent behavioral
marker.

The work fits \emph{Imaging Neuroscience} because it combines open human
neuroimaging, deformable registration, reproducible computational methodology,
and out-of-sample brain--behavior modeling. Code, tests, aggregate results, and
LaTeX sources are publicly available in the
\href{https://github.com/ajoshiusc/ARCDeformationAnalysis}{ARCDeformationAnalysis
repository}; the source dataset is available from OpenNeuro.

OpenAI Codex assisted with code refactoring, statistical implementation,

and accepts responsibility for the submitted code and text. The corresponding
