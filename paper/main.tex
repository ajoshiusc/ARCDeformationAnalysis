\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Purpose:}
Nonlinear registration fields around chronic stroke lesions contain anatomical
signal but also premorbid asymmetry, tissue collapse, and registration error.
We developed a contralateral-normalized lesion-associated deformation proxy and
tested whether it improves held-out prediction of Western Aphasia Battery
Aphasia Quotient (WAB-AQ) beyond conventional lesion features.
\textbf{Materials and Methods:}
From the Aphasia Recovery Cohort, we analyzed one T1-weighted acquisition per
participant. An affine component fitted in the contralesional hemisphere was
removed from the SVReg inverse coordinate map. The residual was contrasted with
its mirrored contralesional homolog and retained only in the lesional
hemisphere. Lesion and inpainting-target voxels were excluded. Magnitude,
radial displacement, and log-Jacobian asymmetry were summarized 3--20~mm from
the lesion. Ridge models were evaluated by 5-fold nested cross-validation
repeated 20 times on identical splits.
\textbf{Results:}
Of \ManifestN{} deformation cases, \ClinicalMatchN{} matched the clinical
table and one failed laterality quality control, leaving \AnalysisN{}
participants. Median near-lesion magnitude was \MagnitudeMedian~mm (IQR
\MagnitudeQOne--\MagnitudeQThree). The conventional lesion model achieved mean
absolute error (MAE) \StandardMAE{} AQ points; adding deformation yielded
\DeformationMAE{}. The participant-level mean MAE advantage was
\DeformationAdvantage{} points (95\% bootstrap CI \DeformationCILow{} to
\DeformationCIHigh{}). Adding deformation after lesion-uncertainty features
gave \AfterUncertaintyAdvantage{} points (CI \AfterUncertaintyCILow{} to
\AfterUncertaintyCIHigh{}).
\textbf{Conclusion:}
The proxy provides an auditable cross-sectional description of lesion-adjacent
registration geometry, but its incremental predictive benefit was not
statistically robust because the primary mean-effect interval included zero.
It must not be interpreted as pressure or physical ground-truth mass effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Spatial normalization of brains with focal lesions is difficult because an
intensity-driven transformation may deform normal anatomy to reduce mismatch at
the lesion \citep{Brett2001,Andersen2010}. Lesion masking and filling can reduce
this bias \citep{Sdika2009}, but the resulting transformation remains a mixture
of global pose, natural anatomical variation, lesion-associated remodeling, and
algorithmic error. These qualifications are especially important in chronic
stroke, where lesions and whole-brain morphology can continue to evolve years
after the event \citep{Seghier2014}.

Deformation-based morphometry uses displacement and transformation Jacobians to
describe local shape or volume differences \citep{Chung2001}. Tumor studies
have related modeled deformation to radiological mass-effect scores
\citep{Zacharaki2009}, but that mechanistic interpretation does not transfer
directly to chronic infarction. Without a premorbid or acute reference scan, a
single chronic image cannot identify the causal displacement produced by the
stroke. A useful cross-sectional descriptor must therefore separate its
measurable geometric definition from an unidentifiable physical claim.

The Aphasia Recovery Cohort (ARC) provides a large, openly released chronic
stroke imaging and behavioral resource \citep{Gibson2024}. Lesion anatomy is a
strong predictor of aphasia severity, while extralesional morphometry may
contain additional information \citep{Teghipco2024}. We asked whether a compact
set of lesion-adjacent deformation summaries adds held-out predictive value
beyond age, assessment delay, lesion size, laterality, and language-network
lesion burden. Our contributions are: (1) a within-participant,
contralateral-normalized field with exact lesional-only support; (2) explicit
registration-sensitivity and folding quality controls; and (3) a
leakage-resistant, repeated nested-CV evaluation designed to support a valid
negative result.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Data and cohort construction}

ARC release 1.0.2 contains de-identified BIDS imaging and participant-level
behavioral variables from chronic stroke survivors \citep{Gibson2024}. WAB-AQ
summarizes spontaneous speech, comprehension, repetition, and naming on a
0--100 scale, with lower values indicating greater impairment
\citep{Kertesz2007}. The source dataset reports informed consent under
University of South Carolina institutional review and exemption of the
anonymized public release from further review \citep{Gibson2024}.

The analysis used one acquisition per participant from the case table paired to
the participant's WAB record. The deformation manifest contained \ManifestN{}
unique cases. Completed deformation, clinical, and optional uncertainty tables
were joined one-to-one by acquisition identifier. Multiple acquisitions per
participant after joining were prohibited. The final cohort had median age at
stroke \AgeMedian{} years (IQR \AgeQOne--\AgeQThree), WAB assessment delay
\WABDaysMedian{} days (IQR \WABDaysQOne--\WABDaysQThree), WAB-AQ \AQMedian{}
(IQR \AQQOne--\AQQThree), and lesion volume \LesionVolumeMedian~mL (IQR
\LesionVolumeQOne--\LesionVolumeQThree). Lesions were left-dominant in
\LeftLesionN{} cases and right-dominant in \RightLesionN{}.

Upstream lesion masks were produced by a full-resolution nnU-Net
\citep{Isensee2021} trained on ATLAS v2 \citep{Liew2022}. Inpainted T1 images
were used only to provide a continuous substrate for BrainSuite v23a processing
\citep{Shattuck2002} and surface-constrained volumetric registration (SVReg)
\citep{Joshi2009}. No generated intensity, label, or morphometric measurement
inside the lesion or dilated inpainting target was treated as biological data.

\subsection{Contralateral-normalized deformation}

Let $F(\mathbf{x})$ be the SVReg inverse coordinate map giving the
subject-voxel coordinate corresponding to atlas voxel $\mathbf{x}$. On valid
contralesional brain voxels more than two voxels from the midline, we fitted
\begin{equation}
 F_A(\mathbf{x}) = M\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}
\end{equation}
by least squares with five iterations of median-absolute-deviation outlier
rejection. The affine-removed residual, expressed in atlas-axis millimeters,
was
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}) = M^{-1}\{F(\mathbf{x})-F_A(\mathbf{x})\}.
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Masked normalized Gaussian smoothing used a 2-mm kernel.

Let $R$ reflect position through the symmetric atlas midline and reverse a
vector's left--right component. The lesion-associated asymmetry field was
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{u}_L(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}) - R\mathbf{u}(R\mathbf{x}).
 \label{eq:asymmetry}
\end{equation}
Both members of every mirrored pair had to lie within valid atlas and mapped
subject brain masks. The lesion, the 3-mm-dilated inpainting target, and its
mirror were excluded. Equation~\ref{eq:asymmetry} was retained only in the
lesional hemisphere; all values in the reference hemisphere were stored as
exact zeros.

Magnitude was $m=\lVert\mathbf{u}_L\rVert$. The gradient of the Euclidean
distance outside the lesion defined outward unit normal $\mathbf{n}$, and
radial displacement was $r=\mathbf{u}_L\cdot\mathbf{n}$ (positive outward,
negative inward). Local volume change was
$J=\det(I+\nabla\mathbf{u})$. For positive paired Jacobians we calculated
$\Delta\log J=\log J(\mathbf{x})-\log J(R\mathbf{x})$. Summaries were retained
in 3--5, 5--10, 10--20, and 20--40~mm shells.

\subsection{Quality control and sensitivity}

Eligible cases required lesion laterality index
$|V_L-V_R|/(V_L+V_R)\geq0.80$, nonpositive-Jacobian fraction no greater than
0.05, and at least 1,000 valid voxels 3--20~mm from the lesion. The direct,
non-inpainted SVReg field was normalized identically when available. The
magnitude of the inpainted-minus-direct field difference was recorded as
registration sensitivity, not as biological deformation.

The predictive analysis used eight prespecified 3--20~mm summaries: median and
95th-percentile magnitude; median and mean-absolute radial displacement;
outward and inward radial integrals; and positive and negative log-Jacobian
integrals. A sensitivity model additionally included median registration
sensitivity and contralateral affine-fit RMSE.

\subsection{Aphasia prediction and inference}

The clinical model contained age at stroke and $\log(1+\text{WAB days})$. The
conventional lesion model added total lesion volume, left and right
language-network lesion burden, and lesion laterality. The primary incremental
model added the eight deformation summaries. Optional models added soft-lesion
and boundary-ambiguity features, with and without deformation.

Within each training fold, missing predictors were median-imputed,
standardized, and fit by ridge regression. A four-fold inner loop selected the
penalty from 17 log-spaced values between $10^{-4}$ and $10^4$ by MAE. A
five-fold shuffled outer loop was repeated 20 times using identical splits for
all models. Repeating every preprocessing and selection step inside the outer
loop limits model-selection bias \citep{Varma2006}.

Performance measures were out-of-fold MAE, RMSE, $R^2$, and Pearson $r$. The
primary effect was each participant's absolute error under the conventional
model minus error under the deformation model, averaged over repeats; positive
values favor deformation. A 5,000-resample participant bootstrap estimated its
95\% CI. Two-sided paired Wilcoxon tests were Holm-adjusted across comparisons
with the conventional model \citep{Holm1979}. Because the signed-rank test and
the bootstrap mean interval address different estimands, predictive superiority
required the prespecified mean-advantage interval to exclude zero.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Cohort flow and deformation characteristics}

Of \ManifestN{} unique deformation cases, \ClinicalMatchN{} matched the
clinical table. One bilateral case failed laterality QC, leaving \AnalysisN{}
participants for every model. Median nonpositive-Jacobian fraction was
\FoldingPctMedian\% (IQR \FoldingPctQOne--\FoldingPctQThree\%).

Across participants, median 3--20~mm magnitude was \MagnitudeMedian~mm (IQR
\MagnitudeQOne--\MagnitudeQThree), median radial displacement was
\RadialMedian~mm (IQR \RadialQOne--\RadialQThree), and mean-absolute radial
displacement was \AbsRadialMedian~mm (IQR
\AbsRadialQOne--\AbsRadialQThree). Median direct-versus-inpainted registration
sensitivity was \SensitivityMedian~mm (IQR
\SensitivityQOne--\SensitivityQThree), giving a proxy-to-sensitivity ratio of
\SignalSensitivityRatioMedian{} (IQR \SignalSensitivityRatioQOne--
\SignalSensitivityRatioQThree). Thus, the deformation scale was comparable to
its preprocessing sensitivity, reinforcing the need for cautious
cross-sectional interpretation.

development case for technical visualization rather than as a representative
participant. The cyan lesion and dashed yellow exclusion target are displayed

\subsection{Held-out AQ prediction}

The clinical-only model performed poorly (MAE \ClinicalMAE{}; $R^2$
\ClinicalRSquared). The conventional lesion model achieved MAE \StandardMAE{},
RMSE \StandardRMSE{}, and $R^2$ \StandardRSquared. Adding deformation reduced
MAE to \DeformationMAE{}, whereas the registration-QC sensitivity model yielded
\DeformationQCMAE{} (Table~\ref{tab:models}; Fig.~\ref{fig:model}).

The primary mean MAE advantage was \DeformationAdvantage{} points (95\%
bootstrap CI \DeformationCILow{} to \DeformationCIHigh{}), and
\DeformationImprovedFraction{} of participants had lower repeated-CV error.
The adjusted signed-rank result was $p_H=\DeformationP$, but the mean-effect CI
crossed zero. Under the prespecified decision rule, deformation therefore did
not establish incremental predictive benefit. Adding registration-QC features
gave a similar advantage of \DeformationQCAdvantage{} points (CI
\DeformationQCCILow{} to \DeformationQCCIHigh{}).

The lesion-uncertainty model had MAE \UncertaintyMAE{}. Adding deformation to
that model produced MAE \CombinedMAE{} and a mean advantage of
\AfterUncertaintyAdvantage{} points (CI \AfterUncertaintyCILow{} to
\AfterUncertaintyCIHigh{}; $p=\AfterUncertaintyP$), providing no evidence of
additive value.

\section{Discussion}

We constructed a fully auditable lesion-associated deformation proxy from
registration fields in a large chronic stroke cohort. The method removes a
contralaterally fitted affine component, subtracts mirrored control geometry,
excludes generated targets and their homologs, and stores the effect only on
the lesional side. These choices prevent a reflected mathematical copy from
being mistaken for contralesional biology and provide explicit spatial support
for every scalar summary.

The prediction experiment yielded a small apparent improvement over a
conventional lesion model, but the primary participant-bootstrap interval
included zero. The significant signed-rank result does not contradict that
conclusion: it concerns ranks of paired errors, whereas the prespecified effect
was their mean difference. A skewed error distribution can produce these two
outcomes. Reporting both, while conditioning the superiority claim on the
mean-effect interval, avoids converting a distributional shift into an
unsupported average-benefit claim.

Deformation added no value after soft lesion and boundary-ambiguity features.
One explanation is informational overlap: deformation integrals and lesion
uncertainty both scale with lesion extent and boundary complexity even after
total volume and laterality are included. Another is measurement noise. Median
proxy magnitude was only modestly larger than median direct-versus-inpainted
registration sensitivity, so improved correspondences and residual algorithmic
variation may obscure smaller behavioral signal.

The field is not physical ground-truth mass effect. Positive radial displacement
does not establish ongoing pressure, and negative displacement can reflect
collapse or ventricular expansion rather than reversal of acute displacement.
The contralateral hemisphere is an internal reference, not a normative atlas;
natural asymmetry and bilateral pathology violate its assumptions. The method
is therefore restricted to strongly unilateral lesions and should be described
as cross-sectional registration geometry.

Limitations include the absence of premorbid or acute reference scans, external
validation, and expert ground truth for the deformation itself. The cohort is
almost entirely left-lesioned because ARC focuses on aphasia. Upstream lesion
delineation and inpainting can influence correspondences despite exclusion of
target voxels. Repeated cross-validation reduces split dependence but does not
replace validation in an independent cohort. Future work should include
longitudinal deformation, non-inpainted and normative controls, tissue-specific
summaries, uncertainty calibration, and preregistered external testing.

\section{Conclusion}

Contralateral normalization yields a transparent, lesional-only description of
chronic stroke registration asymmetry. In ARC, deformation features produced a
small reduction in held-out AQ error, but the primary confidence interval
crossed zero and deformation added nothing after lesion uncertainty. The result
supports the proxy as a research descriptor and does not support a claim of
robust incremental prediction, pressure estimation, or causal mass effect.
